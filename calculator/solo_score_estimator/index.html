Solo Score Estimator

The Solo Score Estimator combines execution, consistency, quality, and goal completion into a single weighted performance score. Each component is rated on a 0–100 scale, and the calculator applies transparent weights to produce an overall result and performance band.

The score can be used for personal reviews, habit tracking, independent project retrospectives, or weekly check-ins. It is most useful when the same definitions and evidence are used repeatedly, allowing changes over time to be compared.

Solo score = Execution × 30% + Consistency × 25% + Quality × 25% + Goal completion × 20%
Bands: Excellent ≥ 85; Strong ≥ 70 and < 85; Developing ≥ 50 and < 70; Needs attention < 50

What the result means

The main result is a weighted score from 0 to 100 that summarizes the four entered dimensions.

This is a self-defined tracking index. It does not represent an external certification or standardized performance assessment.

Given: Execution 75, consistency 80, quality 85, and goal completion 70%.

Calculation: 75 × 0.30 = 22.5; 80 × 0.25 = 20; 85 × 0.25 = 21.25; 70 × 0.20 = 14. Total = 77.75.

Result: The weighted solo score is 77.75, which falls in the Strong band.

Why use weighted components instead of a simple average?

Weights make the relative importance of each dimension explicit. This model gives execution the largest share and goal completion the smallest.

Can I enter ratings with decimals?

Yes. Decimal ratings are accepted when your measurement method supports that precision.

What evidence should support the ratings?

Use completed-task records, time logs, quality checks, or another repeatable source. Unsupported ratings make period-to-period comparisons less reliable.

Can a high quality score offset low completion?

Partly, because all components contribute to the total. However, each dimension has a limited weight and cannot completely replace the others.

Is this the same as a productivity rate?

No. A productivity rate usually compares output with time or another input. This score combines several subjective or measured dimensions.
