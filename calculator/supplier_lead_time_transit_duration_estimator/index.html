Supplier Lead Time Transit Duration Estimator

The Supplier Lead Time Transit Duration Estimator isolates the transportation portion of supplier lead time by adding planned movement time, transfer or handoff delays, and a practical delay allowance. It is useful when buyers, planners, and logistics teams need a consistent transit estimate before setting reorder dates or comparing supplier lanes.

The result focuses on elapsed transit days rather than the full purchase-to-receipt cycle. Keeping processing time separate makes it easier to see whether a long supplier lead time comes from transportation, supplier preparation, or internal receiving work.

  1. Enter the base transit time
    Use the normal elapsed time from supplier dispatch to destination before exceptional delays.

  2. Add transfer delay
    Include expected consolidation, cross-dock, port, terminal, or carrier handoff time that is not already in the base figure.

  3. Set a delay allowance
    Enter a percentage buffer to reflect typical variability on the lane. Use 0% when you want a pure scheduled-time estimate.

  4. Review the estimate
    Use the total transit duration as the transportation component of a broader supplier lead-time plan.

Formula:

Transit duration = (Base transit time + Handoff delay) × (1 + Delay allowance ÷ 100)

Where:

  • Base transit time — planned transportation time in days
  • Handoff delay — additional expected transfer or waiting time in days
  • Delay allowance — percentage buffer applied to the combined scheduled time

Assumptions: The calculator treats the allowance as proportional to the combined base and handoff time. Supplier production, order approval, customs clearance, and receiving time are outside this estimate unless already embedded in an input.

Does this include supplier production time?

No. This estimator is designed for the transit portion after goods are ready to move. Add supplier preparation or manufacturing time separately when building total lead time.

Should weekends be counted?

Use the same day basis as your source data. If carrier schedules are stated in calendar days, enter calendar days; if they are working-day estimates, keep all inputs on a working-day basis.

What should I use for the delay allowance?

Use a value supported by your lane history or planning policy. A stable lane can use a smaller buffer, while a lane with frequent variability may need a larger one.

Can I include customs delay in the handoff field?

You can if customs delay is consistently part of the transit estimate you want to model. For clearer analysis, keep unusually variable customs time separate when possible.

How is this different from total supplier lead time?

Total supplier lead time can include order processing, production, transit, customs, and receiving. This tool intentionally estimates only the transportation-related duration.
