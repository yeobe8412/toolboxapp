Study Reading Calculator

The Study Reading Calculator estimates the time and daily pace required to finish a reading assignment by a deadline. It accounts for pages remaining, reading speed, planned reading days, and a review allowance added to the first-pass reading time.

The output provides total hours, pages per day, minutes per day, and the number of standard study sessions needed. It is useful for textbooks, research packets, novels, and exam reading plans. Reading speed should reflect the type of material because dense technical pages often take substantially longer than familiar prose.

1. Enter pages remaining
Exclude pages already completed unless they need to be reread.

2. Use a realistic reading speed
Measure pages per focused hour for material of similar difficulty.

3. Set available days
Count days on which you can actually read before the deadline.

4. Add review allowance
Reserve a percentage of first-pass time for notes, summaries, or rereading.

5. Choose a session length
Use the standard block length you plan to schedule.

6. Review the daily pace
Use both pages and minutes per day to build the plan.

First-pass hours = Pages ÷ Pages per hour
Review hours = First-pass hours × Review allowance
Total hours = First-pass hours + Review hours
Minutes per day = Total hours × 60 ÷ Reading days

Review allowance is entered as a percentage and converted to a decimal. The calculator assumes a constant reading rate and evenly distributes the workload across the available days.

What the result means

The main result includes both initial reading time and the chosen review allowance.

Figures, exercises, annotation, and unfamiliar vocabulary can reduce the effective pages-per-hour rate.

Given: 240 pages, 30 pages per hour, six reading days, a 20% review allowance, and 45-minute sessions.

Calculation:
First-pass time = 240 ÷ 30 = 8.00 hours
Review time = 8 × 0.20 = 1.60 hours
Total = 9.60 hours
Daily time = 9.60 × 60 ÷ 6 = 96 minutes

Result: Plan about 40 pages and 96 minutes per day, or roughly 12.8 forty-five-minute sessions in total.

How can I measure reading speed?

Read representative material for 20 to 30 focused minutes, count completed pages, and scale the rate to one hour.

Should note-taking time be included?

Include it in the measured reading rate or add it through the review allowance, but avoid counting it twice.

Can I use chapters instead of pages?

Yes, if both the workload and rate use chapters. Pages are usually more precise when chapter lengths vary.

Why are daily pages and daily minutes both shown?

Pages provide a visible progress target, while minutes help reserve calendar time.

Does the estimate include breaks?

No. The time represents focused reading and review; schedule breaks separately.
