Pasture Stocking Yield Forecast Estimator

The Pasture Stocking Yield Forecast Estimator projects the amount of forage that may be available to grazing livestock after accounting for pasture area, expected forage production, and the portion of that forage you plan to utilize. It turns a per-acre yield estimate into a whole-pasture usable-forage figure for stocking and rotation planning.

The calculation distinguishes standing forage production from usable forage. That distinction matters because leaving residual biomass can protect regrowth, soil cover, and future production, while trampling and selective grazing also prevent animals from consuming every pound grown. Use the forecast as a planning estimate and update it with pasture measurements as conditions change.

1. Enter grazeable acreage
Use the area actually available to the stocked group.

2. Estimate forage yield
Enter expected dry-matter production per acre for the relevant growth period.

3. Set planned utilization
Choose the share of standing forage you expect to allocate for animal consumption.

4. Enter grazing duration
This does not change total usable forage, but it converts the forecast to an average daily allocation.

5. Review standing versus usable forage
Use the gap to understand how much biomass remains outside the planned grazing allocation.

Standing forage = Pasture area × Forage yield per acre Usable forage = Standing forage × (Utilization ÷ 100) Average daily usable forage = Usable forage ÷ Grazing days

The model assumes the yield estimate applies uniformly over the entered acreage and does not add regrowth during the grazing period unless that regrowth is already included in the yield input.

What the result means

The main result is the quantity of dry matter allocated as usable forage under the selected utilization percentage.

It is not a direct stocking-rate recommendation because animal intake requirements and allowable residuals vary by class of livestock, forage type, season, and management objective.

Given:

  • Pasture area = 80 ac
  • Expected forage yield = 3,200 lb DM/ac
  • Planned utilization = 45%
  • Grazing period = 30 days

Calculation:
Standing forage = 80 × 3,200 = 256,000 lb DM. Usable forage = 256,000 × 0.45 = 115,200 lb DM. Daily usable forage = 115,200 ÷ 30 = 3,840 lb DM/day.

Result:
Forecast usable forage = 115,200 lb DM.

Interpretation:
Under the selected utilization assumption, 140,800 lb DM remains outside the planned grazing allocation.

Why not use 100% utilization?

Animals rarely consume all standing forage, and leaving residue may be important for plant recovery and soil protection. The appropriate utilization target depends on the forage system and management goal.

Should forage yield be entered as dry matter?

Yes. Dry matter avoids moisture-driven distortion and is the most consistent basis for comparing forage supply with animal intake.

Does the calculator include regrowth during grazing?

Only if you include expected regrowth in the yield-per-acre input. Otherwise, the model treats the entered forage supply as fixed.

Can I use this to determine exactly how many animals to stock?

Not by itself. You also need expected intake per animal, grazing duration, desired residual, and possibly a contingency reserve.

What does unallocated forage mean?

It is the standing forage not included in your planned utilization percentage. It can represent residual biomass, trampling, selectivity, or other forage that is not expected to be consumed.
