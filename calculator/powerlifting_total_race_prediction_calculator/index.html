Powerlifting Total Race Prediction Calculator

The Powerlifting Total Race Prediction Calculator projects a future three-lift total from a current total, a number of training weeks, and an assumed weekly improvement rate. In this context, “race prediction” is treated as competition-outcome projection rather than a literal timed race, because powerlifting performance is judged by successful attempts and total weight.

The calculator is useful for goal setting before a meet or testing day when you want to make the growth assumption explicit instead of simply adding an arbitrary number of kilograms. It compounds the entered weekly rate, so even a small percentage can produce a large projection over long periods. Strength progress is rarely smooth or guaranteed; use conservative assumptions and treat the result as a scenario, not a promise of a meet total.

1. Enter your current total
Use a recent, credible squat-plus-bench-plus-deadlift total under the standard you plan to use at the event.

2. Set the time horizon
Enter the number of training weeks until the target meet or testing date.

3. Choose a weekly rate
Enter the average percentage change you want the scenario to assume. A negative rate can model a decline.

4. Review the projection
The main result compounds the weekly rate across the full time horizon and shows the resulting total.

5. Stress-test the assumption
Try lower and higher rates rather than relying on one optimistic figure, especially for long time horizons.

Projected total = Current total × (1 + Weekly rate ÷ 100)^Weeks Projected gain = Projected total − Current total

Where:
Current total = current squat + bench press + deadlift total in kilograms
Weekly rate = assumed percentage change per week
Weeks = number of compounding periods

Assumptions: This is a scenario model. It assumes a constant compounded rate even though real strength gains are irregular, affected by training age, peaking, body weight, injuries, judging standards, and attempt selection.

What the result means

The result is the total implied by your chosen compounded improvement assumption at the end of the entered number of weeks.

Small weekly percentages compound quickly. Use conservative rates and compare several scenarios.

Given:
Current total: 520 kg
12 weeks to event
Weekly improvement: 0.25%

Calculation:
Growth multiplier = (1 + 0.25/100)^12 = 1.0304
Projected total = 520 × 1.0304 = 535.82 kg
Projected gain = 535.82 − 520 = 15.82 kg

Result:
535.8 kg

Interpretation: Under this constant-growth scenario, the projected event total is about 16 kg above the current total.

Why does this calculator use compound growth?

Compounding makes each week’s percentage apply to the updated total. It is a mathematical scenario, not a claim that strength actually improves smoothly every week.

What weekly rate should I enter?

Use a rate grounded in your own recent trend and keep the time horizon in mind. Advanced lifters often need much smaller assumptions than beginners, but the calculator does not prescribe a universal rate.

Can I use a negative percentage?

Yes. A negative rate can model detraining, a conservative scenario, or a period where performance is expected to decrease.

Does the projection account for peaking?

No. Peaking, tapering, weight cuts, attempt selection, and meet-day conditions are not modeled separately.

Is the projected number an official meet prediction?

No. It is a goal-setting scenario based only on current total, time, and the percentage assumption you provide.
