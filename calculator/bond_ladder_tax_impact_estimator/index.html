Bond Ladder Tax Impact Estimator

The Bond Ladder Tax Impact Estimator approximates how federal and state income taxes can reduce the spendable interest from a taxable bond ladder. It also allows a tax-exempt share so mixed ladders can be modeled without treating every dollar of interest identically.

The output is designed for planning comparisons, not tax filing. Bond tax treatment can differ for municipal obligations, U.S. government obligations, original issue discount, market discount, premium amortization, and holdings inside tax-advantaged accounts. IRS guidance notes that interest is generally taxable unless an exception applies, while some state or local government bond interest may be tax exempt.

1. Enter invested principal
Use the face or market-value amount you want to model as a portfolio-level principal assumption.

2. Enter average yield
Use the expected annual yield for the ladder.

3. Add marginal tax rates
Enter the federal and state/local marginal rates relevant to the taxable interest you are modeling.

4. Set tax-exempt share
If part of the ladder’s interest is exempt under your assumptions, enter that percentage; otherwise leave it at 0%.

5. Compare gross and net income
Review estimated tax cost and after-tax annual interest rather than relying on headline yield alone.

Gross interest = Principal × Yield
Taxable interest = Gross interest × (1 − Exempt share)
Estimated tax = Taxable interest × (Federal rate + State/local rate)
After-tax interest = Gross interest − Estimated tax

This is a simplified marginal-rate model. It does not model deductions, alternative minimum tax, phaseouts, tax brackets, OID, market discount, or security-specific exemptions.

What the result means

The main result estimates annual interest remaining after the modeled income-tax drag.

Use security-specific tax rules for actual decisions; tax-exempt interest can still have reporting or other tax consequences.

Given: $300,000 principal, 5.00% yield, 24% federal rate, 5% state/local rate, and 0% tax-exempt share.

Calculation: Gross interest = $300,000 × 5% = $15,000. Estimated tax = $15,000 × (24% + 5%) = $4,350. After-tax interest = $15,000 − $4,350 = $10,650.

Result: Estimated after-tax annual interest of $10,650.

Interpretation: Under these simplified rates, 29% of the modeled taxable interest is lost to income taxes.

Are all bond ladder payments taxable?

No. Tax treatment depends on the type of bond and the nature of each payment. Principal returned at maturity is not the same as interest income, and some interest may qualify for an exemption.

How should I model municipal bonds?

Use the tax-exempt share only if the interest is exempt under the rules that apply to your situation. Some municipal bond interest can have special federal or state treatment.

Why does the calculator add federal and state rates?

It is a simple marginal-rate approximation for taxable interest. Actual state deductions, federal interactions, and tax brackets can make the effective tax rate different.

What if the bonds are in an IRA or other tax-advantaged account?

This calculator is aimed at currently taxable interest. Tax-deferred or tax-free accounts follow different timing and distribution rules, so the model may not fit them.

Does this include capital gains or losses from selling bonds?

No. It estimates income tax on interest only and does not calculate gains, losses, market discount, or premium adjustments.
