Climbing Grade Recovery Needs Estimator

The Climbing Grade Recovery Needs Estimator combines climbing workload, sleep, finger/forearm soreness, and session intensity into a simple recovery score. It is intended for planning between hard climbing days, especially when projecting, steep bouldering, or repeated high-grade attempts leave local tissues and grip feeling taxed.

Enter the grade index, hard attempts, sleep, and current soreness. The calculator reports a 0–100 recovery-need score and a suggested interval before another similarly demanding session. This does not diagnose tendon or pulley conditions; localized sharp pain, swelling, loss of function, or worsening symptoms need more than a generic recovery estimate.

1. Enter the climb difficulty
Use your established numeric grade index so session comparisons stay consistent.

2. Count hard attempts
Include attempts that significantly loaded fingers, forearms, or pulling muscles.

3. Add recent sleep
Enter the hours slept during the previous night.

4. Rate local soreness
Use a 0–10 rating for ordinary finger and forearm training soreness.

5. Plan the next hard session
Use the score and suggested window as one input alongside pain, skin condition, motivation, and actual movement quality.

Workload = (Grade index ÷ 40) × Hard attempts × 5Sleep modifier = clamp[1 + (7.5 − Sleep hours) × 0.08, 0.80, 1.35]Recovery score = clamp[Workload × Sleep modifier + Soreness × 4, 0, 100]Suggested hours = round(16 + Recovery score × 0.55)

Where:

  • Grade index = normalized climbing difficulty from 1 to 40
  • Hard attempts = demanding attempts in the recent session
  • Sleep hours = hours slept last night
  • Soreness = ordinary finger/forearm soreness from 0 to 10

Assumptions: The model emphasizes hard attempts and local soreness. It cannot distinguish normal training soreness from an injury.

What the result means

The moderate-to-high recovery need is driven by both difficult attempts and noticeable local soreness.

This calculator provides an estimate for planning and comparison; real-world outcomes can vary with conditions and individual factors.

Given:

  • Grade index = 24
  • Hard attempts = 10
  • Sleep = 7 hours
  • Soreness = 5/10

Calculation:
Workload = (24 ÷ 40) × 10 × 5 = 30.0
Sleep modifier = 1 + (7.5 − 7) × 0.08 = 1.04
Recovery score = 30.0 × 1.04 + 5 × 4 = 51.2
Suggested hours = round(16 + 51.2 × 0.55) = 44 hours

Result: 51/100, with about 44 hours suggested.

Interpretation: The moderate-to-high recovery need is driven by both difficult attempts and noticeable local soreness.

Does the suggested window mean I am safe to climb hard afterward?

No. It is a planning estimate, not clearance for training. Your actual symptoms and movement quality matter more than the clock.

What soreness should I enter?

Use ordinary post-training finger and forearm soreness. Do not treat sharp, focal, or worsening pain as a normal soreness score.

Why are hard attempts weighted heavily?

High-intensity attempts can create substantial local stress even when total session time is short, so the model emphasizes attempt difficulty and count.

Can good sleep reduce the score?

Yes, modestly. The sleep modifier can lower the workload contribution when sleep exceeds 7.5 hours, but it cannot erase high soreness.

How is this different from climbing training load?

Training load estimates the work performed. Recovery needs adds sleep and soreness to translate that work into a rest-planning indicator.
