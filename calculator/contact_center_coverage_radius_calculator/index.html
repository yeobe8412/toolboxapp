Contact Center Coverage Radius Calculator

Estimate an equivalent per-zone coverage radius for a contact-center floor from total service area, the number of wireless or voice coverage zones, and a usable-area efficiency factor. The page converts the required area per zone into a circular radius while allowing for overlap and irregular floor geometry.

This can help with early floor-planning discussions for wireless headsets, Wi-Fi voice, or other radio-based contact-center endpoints, but it is not a predictive RF design tool. Final coverage depends on technology, frequency, transmit power, antenna placement, walls, interference, capacity, roaming behavior, and the minimum signal quality required by the devices. Treat the calculated radius as a geometry check, not a deployment specification.

Area per zone = Total service area ÷ Zones
Equivalent radius = √(Area per zone ÷ (π × Efficiency))

Efficiency is a decimal in the formula. This geometry model does not include path-loss equations or radio-specific sensitivity thresholds.

What the result means

The result is the circular-equivalent radius needed for each zone to account for the stated service area and efficiency.

Coverage and capacity are separate design constraints: a zone can have adequate signal but still be overloaded by too many active devices.

Given: 8,000 m² of service area, 8 zones, and 65% usable-area efficiency.

Calculation: Area per zone = 8,000 ÷ 8 = 1,000 m². Radius = √(1,000 ÷ (π × 0.65)) = 22.12 m.

Result: An equivalent planning radius of about 22.1 m per zone.

What does this radius actually represent?

It is the radius of an equivalent circular zone after applying the chosen usable-area efficiency. It is a geometry result, not a predicted signal boundary.

Can this be used for DECT and Wi-Fi alike?

Only as a rough area-planning calculation. Different radio technologies, frequencies, and deployment rules can produce very different practical coverage.

How should I choose the efficiency percentage?

Use it as an explicit planning assumption based on how irregular the floor is and how much overlap or unusable circular area you expect. Validate the result with real design data.

Does a larger radius always reduce the number of access points needed?

Not necessarily. Capacity, roaming, interference, and device density can require more access points even when signal coverage could reach farther.

Should meeting rooms and support areas be included?

Include any spaces where the relevant endpoints must operate. Exclude areas that do not require service if your objective is only the contact-center coverage footprint.
