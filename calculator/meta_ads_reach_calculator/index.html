Meta Ads Reach Calculator

The Meta Ads Reach Calculator estimates the number of unique people exposed to a campaign from total impressions and average frequency. It helps planners separate unique audience delivery from repeated exposures.

Reach is useful for awareness planning, but it does not indicate clicks, conversions, or attention quality. Use the estimate to evaluate audience scale and potential saturation alongside frequency.

Estimated reach = Impressions ÷ Average frequency Audience reach rate = Estimated reach ÷ Audience size × 100

This is a planning approximation. Actual platform reach is deduplicated and may not equal a simple division.

What the result means

The main result approximates unique people reached.

A person may be counted differently across devices or reporting systems.

Given

  • Impressions: 600,000
  • Average frequency: 2.5
  • Target audience: 300,000

Calculation

Reach = 600,000 ÷ 2.5 = 240,000. Reach rate = 240,000 ÷ 300,000 × 100 = 80%.

Result

Estimated reach: 240,000 people; audience reach rate: 80%.

The campaign delivers 2.5 impressions per reached person on average.

Is reach the same as impressions?

No. Reach counts unique people, while impressions count every delivery, including repeated views by the same person.

What frequency should I use?

Use the average frequency from a comparable campaign or a deliberate planning assumption for the same period.

Can estimated reach exceed the audience size?

The simple formula can produce that result when inputs are inconsistent. Treat it as a signal to revisit the audience-size or frequency assumption.

Why can platform-reported totals differ from this estimate?

Attribution windows, deduplication, delayed reporting, modeled conversions, and invalid-traffic adjustments can change the numbers shown in an ad account.

Should I use planned or actual data?

Use planned inputs for forecasting and actual campaign data for performance review. Keep the time period and attribution basis consistent across all fields.
