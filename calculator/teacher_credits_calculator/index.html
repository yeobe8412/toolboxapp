Teacher Credits Calculator

The Teacher Credits Calculator totals the instructional credits represented by a teacher’s course load and converts them into an estimated weekly contact-hour commitment. It is designed for schedules where courses carry different credit values and where one credit corresponds to a defined amount of weekly teaching time.

The result helps compare course assignments, check whether a load is near a departmental target, and estimate the share of a standard full-time teaching load. Credit systems differ across schools, so the calculator uses the credit-to-contact-hour relationship and full-load benchmark entered by the user rather than assuming one universal policy.

1. Enter course count

Count the courses included in the teaching assignment.

2. Set average credits

Use the average credit value per course, or run separate groups when values differ.

3. Define contact time

Enter the weekly contact hours represented by one credit.

4. Enter the full-load benchmark

Use the credit target stated by the institution.

5. Compare the load

Review total credits, contact hours, and the percentage of a full load.

Total credits = Courses × Average credits per course Weekly contact hours = Total credits × Contact hours per credit Full-load percentage = Total credits ÷ Full-load credits × 100

The calculation assumes the entered average credit value is representative of all courses.

What the result means

The main figure is the total credit value of the assigned courses.

Preparation, grading, advising, and administrative duties are not captured by contact credits alone.

Given:

  • 4 courses
  • 3 credits per course
  • 1 contact hour per credit
  • 12 credits as a full load

Calculation:
Total credits = 4 × 3 = 12. Contact hours = 12 × 1 = 12 hours. Load percentage = 12 ÷ 12 × 100 = 100%.

Result:
12 credits and 100% of the stated full load

Interpretation:
Under these assumptions, the assigned courses equal one full teaching load before non-contact duties are considered.

Are credits the same as weekly teaching hours?

Not always. The relationship depends on the institution, so use the contact-hours-per-credit field to reflect local practice.

What if my courses have different credit values?

Calculate groups of courses separately or use a weighted average credit value.

Does a lab credit count differently?

It may. If labs use a different conversion, calculate them separately with their own contact-hours-per-credit value.

Can this determine contract compliance?

It can organize the numbers, but contract rules may include releases, overloads, and non-teaching duties that require separate review.

Why compare with full-load credits?

The comparison shows how the assigned credits relate to a chosen benchmark, making underload or overload easier to spot.
