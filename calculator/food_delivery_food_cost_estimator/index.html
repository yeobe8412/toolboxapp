Food Delivery Food Cost Estimator

Estimate direct food cost for delivery orders by combining the number of orders, average food cost per order, packaging cost, and expected waste or remake allowance. Delivery economics often include costs that dine-in item analysis can overlook, especially disposable packaging and remake losses. This calculator keeps those direct consumable costs together and compares them with delivery sales.

The main result is estimated total direct food-and-packaging cost for the period. Supporting outputs show direct cost as a percentage of sales, adjusted direct cost per order, and contribution per order before labor, delivery-platform commissions, occupancy, and other overhead. Use your own order-level cost data when available because delivery mix can change substantially by channel and daypart.

1. Enter completed delivery orders
Use the number of orders represented by the sales and cost period.

2. Enter food cost per order
Use the weighted average direct ingredient cost for one delivery order.

3. Add packaging cost
Include boxes, bags, cups, utensils, seals, or other disposable packaging you treat as a direct order cost.

4. Enter waste or remake allowance
Use a percentage for expected direct-cost loss from remakes, spoilage, or similar delivery-related waste not already in the base costs.

5. Enter delivery sales and review
Use sales from the same orders and period. Compare total direct cost, cost percentage, and contribution per order before other operating expenses.

Base direct cost per order = Food cost per order + Packaging cost per orderAdjusted direct cost per order = Base direct cost per order × (1 + Waste/remake allowance ÷ 100)Total direct food cost = Delivery orders × Adjusted direct cost per orderDirect cost % of sales = Total direct food cost ÷ Delivery sales × 100

Where:

Food cost per order = weighted average direct ingredient cost for one completed order
Packaging cost per order = direct disposable packaging cost for one order
Waste/remake allowance = additional percentage applied to base direct cost
Delivery sales = sales from the same order population and period

Assumptions: The model applies one average order cost and one waste allowance to all orders. Platform commissions, courier expense, labor, refunds, taxes, and fixed overhead are excluded unless already embedded in the inputs.

What the result means

The direct-cost percentage shows how much delivery sales are absorbed by food, packaging, and the entered waste allowance. Contribution per order is the amount remaining after those direct costs only.

If refunded or canceled orders create material food cost, account for them in the waste/remake allowance or add their consumed cost to the average food cost.

Given:
680 completed delivery orders
$9.20 food cost per order
$1.35 packaging cost per order
3% waste/remake allowance
$23,800 delivery sales

Calculation:
Base direct cost = $9.20 + $1.35 = $10.55 per order
Adjusted cost = $10.55 × 1.03 = $10.8665 per order
Total direct cost = 680 × $10.8665 = $7,388.22
Direct cost % = $7,388.22 ÷ $23,800 × 100 = 31.04%
Average sales per order = $23,800 ÷ 680 = $35.00
Contribution per order = $35.00 − $10.8665 = $24.1335

Result:
Estimated direct food cost = $7,388.22

Interpretation:
Direct food and packaging cost consumes about 31.04% of delivery sales, leaving approximately $24.13 per order before labor, commissions, occupancy, and other costs.

Should delivery-platform commission be included?

Not in the food-cost inputs. Platform commission is a channel or selling expense and is better analyzed separately unless you intentionally want a broader direct-order-cost model.

How do I estimate average food cost for mixed orders?

Use a weighted average based on the actual order mix, or calculate high-volume menu groups separately and combine the results.

Should packaging be marked up by the waste allowance too?

This model applies the waste/remake allowance to both food and packaging because a remake may consume both. If only food is affected in your process, enter a lower blended allowance or calculate the components separately.

What if delivery sales include tax or tips?

For a cleaner operating ratio, use the revenue amount your business recognizes from the food delivery sale and exclude pass-through taxes or customer tips when they are not business revenue.

How is this different from dine-in food cost?

The core food-cost logic is similar, but delivery often adds packaging and channel-specific remake or loss patterns. This calculator includes packaging explicitly while still excluding labor and platform fees.
