Cat Food Break-Even Price Estimator

The Cat Food Break-Even Price Estimator compares package sizes on an equal weight basis. Starting with the price and weight of a reference package, it calculates the maximum price a candidate package can cost while matching the same price per kilogram.

This is useful when bag or case sizes differ enough that shelf prices are hard to compare directly. The break-even result is a neutral comparison point: a candidate price below it has a lower unit cost, while a price above it has a higher unit cost. The calculation does not account for coupons, taxes, shipping, spoilage, or differences in formulation and nutritional value.

If the actual candidate price is below the break-even result, its price per kilogram is lower than the reference package.

Reference unit price = Reference price ÷ Reference weight
Candidate break-even price = Reference unit price × Candidate weight

Both package weights must represent the same type of quantity. This comparison evaluates price per weight only, not nutrient density or feeding cost per day.

What the result means

The break-even price is the candidate package price that produces exactly the same cost per kilogram as the reference package.

Compare products on other attributes as well when ingredients, calorie density, package waste, or storage life differ.

Given

  • Reference: $28.99 for 4.5 kg
  • Candidate size: 6.8 kg

Calculation

$28.99 ÷ 4.5 = $6.4422 per kg. $6.4422 × 6.8 = $43.807.

Result

About $43.81.

A 6.8 kg candidate below about $43.81 is cheaper per kilogram than the reference package; above that price it is more expensive per kilogram.

Is the lowest unit price always the best value?

Not necessarily. Formula, calorie density, your cat’s tolerance, package freshness, and waste can matter more than price per kilogram alone.

Should sale discounts be included?

Yes, if you want to compare what you will actually pay. Enter the after-discount reference price before taxes or shipping unless you also plan to include those costs consistently.

Can I compare cans with dry food bags?

Only if weight is a meaningful basis for your decision. Wet and dry foods can differ greatly in moisture and energy density, so cost per day may be more informative.

What does it mean if the actual candidate price equals the result?

The two packages have the same price per kilogram based on the entered values.

How is this different from a yearly cost estimator?

Break-even price compares package unit cost at one point in time. A yearly cost estimator incorporates ongoing consumption to estimate spending over a longer period.
