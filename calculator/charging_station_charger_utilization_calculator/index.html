Charging Station Charger Utilization Calculator

This calculator estimates charger utilization for a public or workplace charging station by comparing occupied charging time with the total time that installed connectors are available. It converts sessions, average session length, connector count, and operating hours into a utilization percentage.

The result is useful for capacity planning. Low utilization may indicate spare capacity, while a sustained high percentage can signal queues, limited scheduling flexibility, or a need for additional ports. The tool also reports daily occupied connector-hours and remaining connector-hours so operators can see the workload behind the percentage.

  1. Enter daily sessions. Use the average number of charging sessions completed or expected in one day.
  2. Set session duration. Enter the average plug-in time in minutes, including charging and any typical post-charge dwell time.
  3. Enter connector capacity. Count connectors that can serve vehicles at the same time.
  4. Set available hours. Use the number of hours each connector is accessible per day.
  5. Review utilization. Compare occupied, available, and unused connector-hours with the utilization percentage.
Utilization (%) = (Sessions × Average session minutes ÷ 60) ÷ (Connectors × Available hours) × 100

Occupied connector-hours measure total daily charging time across all sessions. Available connector-hours equal the number of connectors multiplied by the hours each connector can be used. A value above 100% means the entered demand exceeds stated capacity and would require overlapping demand, longer operating hours, or more connectors.

What the result means

Use the result to compare scenarios under the assumptions entered. It is an estimate, not a guaranteed real-world outcome.

Update the inputs with measured vehicle, charger, tariff, and operating data whenever available.

Given: 18 sessions, 95 minutes per session, 4 connectors, and 24 available hours per connector.

Calculation: Occupied hours = 18 × 95 ÷ 60 = 28.5 hours. Available hours = 4 × 24 = 96 hours. Utilization = 28.5 ÷ 96 × 100 = 29.69%.

Result: Daily charger utilization is about 29.7%, leaving 67.5 unused connector-hours under these assumptions.

Can utilization exceed 100%?

Yes, but it means the entered daily charging demand is greater than the capacity defined by connectors and available hours. Treat it as a capacity shortfall rather than literal simultaneous use.

Should dwell time be included in session duration?

Include it when a vehicle normally occupies the connector after active charging ends. Excluding dwell time can understate real-world occupancy.

What time period should I use?

Use one consistent day or an average day. For seasonal sites, calculate representative peak and off-peak days separately.

Does high utilization always mean another charger is needed?

Not necessarily. Scheduling, pricing, load sharing, and reducing unnecessary dwell time may improve access before hardware is added.

How is utilization different from energy throughput?

Utilization measures time occupancy. Energy throughput measures kilowatt-hours delivered, so a charger can have high time utilization but modest energy delivery if charging power is low.
