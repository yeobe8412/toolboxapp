WooCommerce Shipping Calculator

The WooCommerce Shipping Calculator estimates what one shipment costs the store and whether the amount charged to the customer covers that cost. It combines carrier postage, packaging, handling labor, insurance, surcharges, and an optional free-shipping subsidy, then compares the total with shipping revenue.

Use it to review flat-rate shipping, free-shipping thresholds, handling charges, or zone-based pricing. The calculator does not quote a live carrier rate; enter a carrier quote or historical average that matches the package, destination, and service level being modeled.

1. Obtain a representative postage amount
Use a live quote or historical average for the package weight, dimensions, zone, and service level.

2. Add packaging and surcharges
Include boxes, mailers, void fill, insurance, residential fees, and other shipment-specific charges.

3. Value handling labor
Enter minutes per order and an hourly labor rate to convert handling time into cost.

4. Enter the customer charge
Use zero for free shipping or the actual amount collected for a flat-rate policy.

5. Review cost recovery
Compare net cost per order and the period total, then set a charge or product-price subsidy that fits your strategy.

Handling labor cost = Handling minutes ÷ 60 × Hourly labor rate
Total shipping cost = Postage + Packaging + Labor + Insurance + Surcharges
Net shipping cost = Total shipping cost − Shipping charged to customer
Recovery rate = Shipping charged ÷ Total shipping cost × 100

Where:

  • Total shipping cost: store cost for one representative shipment
  • Net shipping cost: portion not recovered through the explicit shipping charge
  • Orders: shipment count used to scale the per-order result

Assumptions: Carrier postage and other inputs are averages for the shipment type being modeled; taxes and payment fees on shipping revenue are excluded.

What the result means

Net shipping cost is the amount the store absorbs after the explicit customer shipping charge.

Live carrier pricing, dimensional weight, zones, and negotiated rates should be reflected in the postage input.

Given:

  • Carrier postage: $9.40
  • Packaging: $1.10
  • Handling: 8 minutes at $18/hour
  • Insurance: $0
  • Surcharge: $0.75
  • Customer shipping charge: $7.99
  • Comparison period: 100 orders

Calculation:
Labor = 8 ÷ 60 × 18 = $2.40. Total shipping cost = 9.40 + 1.10 + 2.40 + 0 + 0.75 = $13.65. Net shipping cost = 13.65 − 7.99 = $5.66. Period cost = 5.66 × 100 = $566.00.

Result:
$5.66 net shipping cost per order.

Interpretation:
The store subsidizes about 41.5% of the modeled shipment cost through product margin or other revenue.

Does this calculate a live carrier rate?

No. Enter a current quote or a reliable average from your shipping records.

How should free shipping be modeled?

Set the customer charge to $0. The displayed net cost is the amount that product margin or another subsidy must absorb.

Should warehouse rent be included?

Only include an allocated amount if you want a fully loaded fulfillment estimate. The default fields focus on shipment-level costs.

Why include handling labor?

Packing, labeling, and dispatch take staff time even when postage is purchased automatically. Omitting labor can understate fulfillment cost.

How can I set a flat shipping charge?

Use the break-even charge as a cost-recovery reference, then adjust for competitive positioning, free-shipping thresholds, and payment fees assessed on shipping revenue.
