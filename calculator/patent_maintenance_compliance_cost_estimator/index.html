Patent Maintenance Compliance Cost Estimator

Estimate patent maintenance compliance spending by separating internal labor, official fees, external services, and overhead. It is designed for planning scenarios where the user can supply the current fees and service rates that actually apply.

That input-driven approach matters because government fee schedules, entity status, filing choices, and outside counsel or vendor charges can change. The calculator separates internal labor from pass-through fees and external services so teams can see which cost driver matters most and update only the assumptions that change.

1. Enter the filing volume
Provide the number of maintenance events expected in the budgeting period.

2. Estimate internal effort
Enter the average staff hours required per item and the loaded hourly cost for the personnel doing the work.

3. Enter current official fees
Use the official fee that applies to your filing, class, entity status, or maintenance event. Check the current authority rather than relying on an old fee schedule.

4. Add external service costs
Include outside counsel, docketing, vendor, or agent cost per item when applicable.

5. Include shared overhead
Add fixed program costs that are not already captured in per-item assumptions.

6. Review total and unit cost
Use the total for budget planning and the per-item figure for scenario comparison.

Internal labor = Volume × Hours per item × Loaded hourly cost Official fees = Volume × Official fee per item External services = Volume × External cost per item Total cost = Internal labor + Official fees + External services + Program overhead Cost per item = Total cost ÷ Volume

The model treats each entered per-item amount as an average. If fees differ by class, entity status, filing type, or event, run separate scenarios or use a volume-weighted average.

What the result means

The result is the estimated total cost of the entered compliance workload and assumptions.

Official fees and legal requirements can change. Confirm current amounts and obligations with the relevant authority and qualified professionals before filing or payment.

Given
Volume: 24 items
Internal effort: 0.8 hours per item at $68.00/hour
Official fee: $1000.00 per item
External service: $180.00 per item
Program overhead: $500

Calculation
Internal labor = 24 × 0.8 × $68.00 = $1,305.60
Official fees = 24 × $1000.00 = $24,000.00
External services = 24 × $180.00 = $4,320.00
Total = $1,305.60 + $24,000.00 + $4,320.00 + $500 = $30,125.60

Result
The estimated program cost is $30,125.60. Replace the example fee assumptions with the current amount that applies to the specific filing or maintenance action.

Why is the official fee an input instead of a fixed amount?

Government fee schedules and fee categories can change, and the amount may depend on filing type, class, entity status, or other facts. Entering the current applicable fee keeps the model usable across scenarios.

What should the loaded hourly cost include?

Use the organization’s preferred fully loaded labor cost when available. It can include wages plus employer costs and allocated overhead, but avoid double-counting overhead that you enter separately.

Should outside counsel costs be entered per item?

Use a per-item average when that is how you budget the work. If outside charges are fixed, enter them in program overhead instead or convert them to a defensible per-item average.

Can the calculator predict the exact amount I will pay?

No. It is a budgeting model based on your assumptions. Actual costs depend on the specific filing, official fee schedule, professional services used, corrections, extensions, and other case-specific events.

How should I model several fee tiers?

Run one scenario for each tier and add the results, or enter a volume-weighted average fee if you only need a single planning estimate. Separate scenarios are easier to audit later.
