Dairy Herd Feed Conversion Calculator

This calculator compares a dairy herd's daily dry matter intake with daily milk output and reports feed conversion as pounds of dry matter consumed per pound of milk produced. It also shows the inverse milk-to-feed efficiency so managers can view the same relationship in the format they commonly track.

Use herd totals from the same time period and on consistent weight bases. The ratio is an operational indicator, not a complete measure of profitability or animal performance: milk components, body-weight change, stage of lactation, forage quality, health, and feed cost can all affect how the result should be interpreted.

1. Enter dry matter intake
Use total herd dry matter intake for a clearly defined day or averaging period.

2. Enter milk output
Use total milk weight for exactly the same herd and time basis.

3. Check unit consistency
Keep both inputs in pounds; do not mix liquid volume with milk weight.

4. Review feed conversion
The main result is pounds of dry matter intake per pound of milk.

5. Review reciprocal efficiency
Use the milk-per-pound-of-DMI figure in the breakdown when that is the preferred herd metric.

Feed conversion ratio = Dry matter intake ÷ Milk output
Milk feed efficiency = Milk output ÷ Dry matter intake

Where:

  • Dry matter intake: entered in lb/day
  • Milk output: entered in lb/day

Assumptions: inputs describe the same system, batch, or planning period, and units are used exactly as labeled. The model is intentionally simple and does not add unentered losses, fees, or biological effects.

What the result means

The main result shows pounds of dry matter intake per pound of milk output; the reciprocal shows pounds of milk per pound of dry matter intake.

Compare values only when intake and milk are measured over the same period and for a comparable group of animals.

Given:

  • Dry matter intake = 6,200 lb/day
  • Milk output = 10,540 lb/day

Calculation:
FCR = 6,200 ÷ 10,540 = 0.5882 lb DMI/lb milk; Efficiency = 10,540 ÷ 6,200 = 1.70 lb milk/lb DMI

Result:
Feed conversion ratio ≈ 0.59 lb DMI/lb milk

Interpretation:
The herd used about 0.59 pound of dry matter for each pound of milk, equivalent to roughly 1.70 pounds of milk per pound of dry matter intake.

Why use dry matter intake instead of as-fed weight?

Dry matter removes the effect of feed moisture and creates a more consistent basis for comparing feed consumed with milk produced.

What does a lower feed conversion ratio mean here?

It means fewer pounds of dry matter were consumed per pound of milk produced. Interpretation should still consider milk components, body condition, health, and stage of lactation.

Why is milk feed efficiency also shown?

Dairy operations often express the relationship as pounds of milk per pound of dry matter intake. It is the reciprocal of the feed-conversion ratio and may be more familiar for herd benchmarking.

Can I compare fresh cows with late-lactation cows directly?

The arithmetic is valid, but biological context differs. Comparisons are more useful for similar groups or when stage of lactation and body-weight change are considered.

Should milk be entered in gallons?

No. Enter milk as weight in pounds so it is directly comparable with dry matter intake by weight. Convert any volume measurement to milk weight before using the calculator.
