Sauna Session Treatment Cost Estimator

Estimate the cost of delivering one sauna session treatment by combining consumables, staff time, equipment allowance, and allocated overhead. The calculator turns labor minutes into a dollar cost and shows how each component contributes to the total service cost.

For a sauna studio, this can support pricing reviews, membership design, and operational budgeting. It is a cost model rather than a clinical assessment, and it is most useful when the same costing rules are applied consistently across sessions and over time.

Labor cost = (Staff minutes ÷ 60) × Loaded hourly staff cost
Treatment cost = Consumables + Labor cost + Equipment allowance + Allocated overhead

Each input is expressed on a per-treatment basis except staff time and the hourly labor rate. The model does not include membership acquisition cost or profit margin unless you incorporate them into overhead.

What the result means

The main result is the estimated economic cost to deliver one treatment under the entered assumptions.

Use consistent costing rules when comparing multiple wellness services.

Given: One sauna session treatment uses $6 in supplies, requires 20 staff minutes at $27/hour loaded labor, carries a $3 equipment allowance, and receives $7 of overhead.

Calculation: Labor = (20 ÷ 60) × $27 = $9.00. Total = $6 + $9 + $3 + $7 = $25.00.

Result: Estimated treatment cost = $25.00.

This is the modeled resource cost per completed session before markup or target profit.

Should setup and cleanup time be included in staff minutes?

Include it when that paid time is directly tied to delivering the session and you want the cost estimate to capture it.

How do I estimate equipment cost per treatment?

Allocate the equipment cost you want recognized across the number of treatments you expect it to support, and use that per-treatment amount as the equipment allowance.

Does the total include profit?

No. The result estimates cost. A selling price or membership allocation would need to exceed cost by whatever margin is required for your business model.

Can I leave a cost component at zero?

Yes. If a component does not apply or is intentionally excluded, enter zero; just keep the scope consistent when comparing results.

How is this different from a service capacity estimator?

Treatment cost measures resource cost per completed session, while capacity estimates how many sessions can be delivered over a period.
