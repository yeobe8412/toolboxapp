Door Replacement Labor Hours Calculator

This calculator estimates the total person-hours required to replace a group of doors and converts that workload into an approximate crew duration. It is useful for homeowners, property managers, and contractors who need a quick labor planning figure before scheduling installers or comparing labor quotes.

The estimate separates per-door installation time from project-wide setup and cleanup time. Because field conditions vary—especially when frames are out of square, trim must be repaired, or hardware needs modification—the result is best used as a planning baseline rather than a guaranteed job duration.

1. Count the doors
Enter the number of door units included in the same replacement project.

2. Set labor per door
Enter the expected person-hours for removing the old door, fitting the replacement, installing hardware, and completing normal trim work.

3. Add shared labor
Include setup, material staging, protection, cleanup, and other labor that applies to the project as a whole.

4. Enter crew size
Use the number of people who will work on the job at the same time.

5. Review workload and duration
Use total person-hours for labor estimating and crew duration for scheduling.

Direct labor hours = Door count × Hours per door
Total person-hours = Direct labor hours + Setup and cleanup hours
Crew duration = Total person-hours ÷ Crew size

Where:

  • Door count — number of doors
  • Hours per door — person-hours required for one door
  • Setup and cleanup hours — shared person-hours for the project
  • Crew size — number of workers

Assumptions: Crew members are assumed to share the workload evenly. Travel, permit delays, drying time, and unexpected carpentry repairs are not included unless you add them to the labor inputs.

What the result means

Use the main result as a planning estimate based on the values entered above. Recalculate whenever project scope, unit costs, productivity, or savings assumptions change.

Actual project requirements can differ because of site conditions, product specifications, workmanship, local pricing, and supplier packaging.

Given:

  • 8 doors
  • 2.25 person-hours per door
  • 3 shared setup/cleanup hours
  • 2-person crew

Calculation:
Direct labor = 8 × 2.25 = 18.0 person-hours
Total labor = 18.0 + 3 = 21.0 person-hours
Crew duration = 21.0 ÷ 2 = 10.5 hours
8-hour crew days = 10.5 ÷ 8 = 1.31 days

Result: 21.0 person-hours, or about 10.5 clock hours for a two-person crew.

Interpretation: The project carries 21 hours of labor cost even though two installers can complete that workload in roughly 1.3 standard workdays if work proceeds continuously.

Why are person-hours different from elapsed hours?

Person-hours measure total labor effort. Elapsed hours divide that effort among the crew, so two workers can complete 20 person-hours in about 10 working hours if they can work efficiently in parallel.

What should I include in hours per door?

Include removal, fitting, fastening, hardware installation, basic trim work, and normal cleanup attributable to each door. Put project-wide staging or final cleanup in the separate setup field.

Should difficult doors use the same rate as simple doors?

No. Exterior doors, fire-rated assemblies, damaged jambs, and nonstandard openings often need more labor than straightforward interior replacements. Use a blended average only if it reasonably represents the mix.

Does a larger crew always shorten the project proportionally?

Not necessarily. The calculation assumes even workload sharing, but tight spaces, sequential tasks, and limited tools can reduce the benefit of adding workers.

Can I use this result to compare contractor quotes?

Yes, as a rough benchmark. Divide a labor quote by the estimated person-hours to see the implied labor cost per person-hour, while remembering that quotes may also include overhead, travel, supervision, and risk allowances.
