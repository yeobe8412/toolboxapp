Monthly Deadline Planner

The Monthly Deadline Planner converts a set of monthly deliverables into a simple pace target. Enter the number of deadlines, the working days available, and a buffer for reviews or unexpected delays to see how frequently work should be completed.

It is useful for people managing reports, client deliverables, editorial calendars, compliance submissions, or any recurring workload with several due dates in one month. The result helps you judge whether the calendar is comfortably spaced or likely to become compressed near month-end.

Usable days = Working days − Buffer days Days per deadline = Usable days ÷ Number of deadlines Deadlines per 5-day week = Deadlines ÷ Usable days × 5

The model assumes deadlines are similar enough to summarize with an average effort. It does not account for fixed due dates or dependencies between tasks.

What the result means

A higher days-per-deadline value means more scheduling room. A value below 1 means more than one deadline must be completed on an average working day.

Use the estimate as a pacing guide, then place any immovable due dates first.

Given: 12 deadlines, 22 working days, 3 buffer days, and 4 hours per deadline.

Calculation: Usable days = 22 − 3 = 19. Days per deadline = 19 ÷ 12 = 1.58. Deadlines per week = 12 ÷ 19 × 5 = 3.16. Total effort = 12 × 4 = 48 hours.

Result: Complete roughly one deadline every 1.58 working days, or about 3.16 per five-day week.

Should I count weekends as working days?

Only count weekends when you genuinely plan to work on them. The tool uses the working-day figure directly.

What belongs in the buffer?

Include days reserved for approvals, rework, sick leave, or unexpected priority changes.

What if deadlines require very different effort?

Use a weighted average for hours per deadline, or split the month into groups of similar work.

Why can the result be below one day?

That indicates the plan requires multiple completions on some working days.

How is this different from a schedule planner?

This calculator measures pace and workload density; a schedule planner places specific tasks onto dates.
